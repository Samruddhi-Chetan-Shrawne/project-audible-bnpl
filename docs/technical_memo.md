## Overview

The project tests whether Buy Now Pay Later delinquency rates lead credit card delinquency rates at the US consumer level. The analysis pulls two time series from public sources, computes a comparable 30+ day delinquency rate on both, and tests the lead-lag relationship through peak comparison and cross-correlation across 24 quarters.

## Data sources and provenance

### FRED, Federal Reserve Economic Data

All FRED series pulled on October 26, 2026 using the public CSV download endpoint `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv`. No API key required.

| Series ID | Description | Frequency | Units |
|---|---|---|---|
| DRCCLACBS | Delinquency Rate on Credit Card Loans, All Commercial Banks | Quarterly | Percent, seasonally adjusted |
| CORCCACBS | Charge-Off Rate on Credit Card Loans, All Commercial Banks | Quarterly | Percent, annualized |
| PSAVERT | Personal Saving Rate | Monthly | Percent |
| REVOLSL | Revolving Consumer Credit Owned and Securitized | Monthly | Millions of dollars |
| TOTALSL | Total Consumer Credit Owned and Securitized | Monthly | Millions of dollars |

Date range for all series: January 1, 2019 to June 30, 2026. Raw pulls saved to `data/raw/<series_id>.csv`. Credit card series are already quarterly in FRED. Monthly series were resampled to quarter end.

### SEC EDGAR, Affirm Holdings

All Affirm Holdings filings pulled on October 26, 2026 via SEC EDGAR's public submissions endpoint `https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001820953.json`. Compliance with SEC's `User-Agent` policy was handled through a descriptive header identifying the researcher and email.

Scope: 10-K, 10-Q, and 8-K filings filed since January 1, 2021 (post-IPO). 83 filings downloaded, index saved to `data/raw/affirm/affirm_filings_index.csv` (a copy is committed at `data/raw/affirm_filings_index.csv`; the filing HTML itself is not committed and is regenerated by the script) with filing date, report date, form type, accession number, primary document name, SEC URL, and local path. One 8-K (accession 0001820953-22-000002) returned a 503 error at download time and was skipped.

Delinquency data was extracted from each 10-K and 10-Q (23 filings total). Each filing contains the aging analysis table of amortized cost basis of loans held for investment by delinquency status, broken into buckets: Current to 3 days past due, 4 to 29 days past due, 30 to 59 days past due, 60 to 89 days past due, and 90 to 119 days past due. Each 10-Q discloses two balance sheet dates (current quarter end and prior fiscal year end), so 23 filings yielded 24 unique quarter observations after deduplication.

## Pipeline

The pipeline is six Python scripts run in sequence. Running all six end-to-end takes under three minutes on a stock laptop.

```
scripts/pull_fred.py
    IN:  Nothing. Hits FRED endpoints directly.
    OUT: data/raw/<5 FRED CSVs>
         data/processed/fred_quarterly.csv (30 quarters, 5 series)

scripts/fetch_affirm_filings.py
    IN:  Nothing. Hits SEC EDGAR directly.
    OUT: data/raw/affirm/<accession>/<primary_doc> (83 HTML files)
         data/raw/affirm/affirm_filings_index.csv (83 rows)

scripts/parse_affirm_delinquency.py
    IN:  data/raw/affirm/affirm_filings_index.csv and the HTML files
    OUT: data/processed/affirm_quarterly.csv (24 quarters)

scripts/chart_lead_lag.py
    IN:  data/processed/fred_quarterly.csv, data/processed/affirm_quarterly.csv
    OUT: outputs/chart_lead_lag.png

scripts/lead_lag.py
    IN:  data/processed/fred_quarterly.csv, data/processed/affirm_quarterly.csv
    OUT: Printed table of Pearson correlation at lags 0 through 10

scripts/build_dashboard_data.py
    IN:  data/processed/fred_quarterly.csv, data/processed/affirm_quarterly.csv,
         the filing index, and the lag functions in lead_lag.py
    OUT: dashboard/data.js (the single data model every dashboard page reads,
         stamped with build time and pipeline commit)
```

Each raw filing is left on disk after processing so a reviewer can trace any derived number back to a specific table in a specific SEC document.

## Methodology

### Why 30+ day delinquency rate

Marvin's Phase 1 critique identified that charge-off rates are not consistently defined across BNPL and credit cards. Credit card issuers charge off at 180 days past due under FFIEC rules. Affirm's installment loans charge off at 120 days past due. Afterpay's pay-in-four product charges off even sooner. A direct charge-off comparison would compare different definitions.

30+ day delinquency is more consistently defined across the two products because both report the same aging bucket structure for regulatory and accounting purposes. The denominator is total outstanding principal on both sides. This is the "comparable loss measure" the Phase 1 feedback asked for.

### What was tried and rejected

**Klarna.** The original Phase 1 pitch included Klarna. Klarna does not file 10-K or 10-Q with the SEC. Klarna IPO'd on NYSE in September 2025 with an F-1 and limited US public filings. We dropped Klarna rather than mix the F-1 numbers with Swedish parent filings from Finansinspektionen.

**Block (Afterpay) and PayPal (Pay in 4).** Both file 10-Ks and 10-Qs with the SEC, but their BNPL businesses sit inside segment-level disclosures that report dollar losses rather than delinquency buckets. We considered adding them to strengthen the "BNPL as category" claim and chose not to for this submission. Their inclusion would require defending why segment-level dollar losses are comparable to Affirm's aged receivables, which is a separate methodological question. We flag it in the position memo as a limitation and a direction for future work.

**Longer time series.** Affirm's December 2020 10-Q (its first) reports delinquency buckets on unpaid principal balance rather than amortized cost. We kept the row with a `basis` flag column rather than drop it, after confirming that totals match across filings that report the same date on different bases.

### Why cross-correlation at multiple lags

Peak-to-peak comparison alone is a one-point test. Two humps at different dates can be a coincidence of pandemic and recovery timing rather than a real leading relationship. We computed Pearson correlation between the two 30+ DPD series at lags 0 through 10 quarters where lag N means Affirm leads cards by N quarters.

We also computed the same correlation on quarter-over-quarter changes. Correlation on levels can be inflated when two series drift in the same direction over the sample. The changes test strips out that shared drift. Both tests peak at a lag of 7 to 8 quarters, which rules out spurious drift-matching as the sole explanation.

### Robustness checks

`build_dashboard_data.py` runs a further set of checks and reports each as a claim, a test that would break it, the result, and a verdict (Hold or Narrow) in the dashboard's position ledger.

| Check | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Levels cross-correlation | Best lead 8 quarters, r = 0.76 (n = 16) | Hold |
| Quarter-over-quarter changes | 7 quarters, r = 0.62 (n = 15) | Hold |
| Prewhitened (AR(1) removed) | 7 quarters, r = 0.58 (n = 15) | Hold |
| Year-over-year changes (seasonality control) | 7 quarters, r = 0.75 (n = 13) | Hold |
| Drop one quarter at a time | Lead stays 8 in all 24 runs | Hold |
| Exclude 2020 | 8 quarters, r = 0.83 (n = 14) | Hold |
| 90+ day delinquency instead of 30+ | 7 quarters, r = 0.67 (n = 17) | Hold |
| Card charge-offs (CORCCACBS) instead of delinquency | 9 quarters, r = 0.79 (n = 15) | Hold |
| Phase-randomized null across all 11 lags (10,000 runs) | Random look-alikes match 5.4% of the time | Narrow |
| Autocorrelation-adjusted 95% interval (Pyper and Peterman 1998) | About 7 independent pairs; range +0.03 to +0.96 | Narrow |
| Trough timing | Troughs only 3 quarters apart (Q4 2020, Q3 2021) | Narrow |
| 0.50-point BNPL trigger | Fired Q4 2021, 10 quarters early; 1 false alarm (Q2 2024) | Narrow |
| Affirm post-2022 decline | Late balances rose $102M to $238M while the book grew 3.6x | Narrow |

The direction and size of the lead survive every transformation. The checks marked Narrow limit how strongly the finding can be stated: the result sits at the edge of conventional significance once autocorrelation and multiple lags are accounted for, and the falling Affirm rate after 2022 partly reflects a growing denominator.

## Reproducibility

### Environment

- Python 3.11 or later (tested on 3.12)
- Packages pinned in `requirements.txt`: pandas, numpy, requests, matplotlib
- Install: `python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && pip install -r requirements.txt`

### Run order

```
python scripts/pull_fred.py
python scripts/fetch_affirm_filings.py
python scripts/parse_affirm_delinquency.py
python scripts/chart_lead_lag.py
python scripts/lead_lag.py
python scripts/build_dashboard_data.py
```

Then open `dashboard/index.html` in a browser. It runs offline; Plotly is vendored in `dashboard/vendor/`.

### Key parameters

- FRED date range: hardcoded 2019-01-01 to 2026-06-30 in `pull_fred.py`. The analysis window (2020-06-30 to 2026-06-30) is set by `START` and `END` in `lead_lag.py`, which `build_dashboard_data.py` imports.
- SEC User-Agent email: hardcoded in `fetch_affirm_filings.py`. Any reviewer running the script should replace with their own email per SEC's data access policy.
- Affirm CIK: 0001820953, hardcoded. Would need to change for other issuers.

### Validation checks built into the scripts

- `parse_affirm_delinquency.py` asserts that for each table, the five delinquency buckets sum to the reported total within a 2 thousand dollar rounding tolerance.
- `chart_lead_lag.py` prints the peak quarter and peak value for each series so a reviewer can cross-check against the chart.
- `lead_lag.py` prints the correlation at each lag with the sample size.

### Known caveat on the Affirm earliest row

The December 2020 10-Q uses unpaid principal balance rather than amortized cost. This row is flagged in the output CSV with `basis = hfi_hfs_upb`. The total is comparable to later filings' amortized cost totals, but the denominator is a slightly different definition. A conservative analyst could drop this row at a cost of one quarter of early history.

## Limitations

1. **Single credit cycle.** The analysis window covers one rise-and-fall in each series. The 7 to 8 quarter lead is defensible but not proven across cycles. A second cycle would materially test the finding.
2. **One lender versus system-wide bank data.** Affirm is one issuer. The FRED credit card series covers all commercial banks. The two are not the same unit of observation. The lead-lag relationship could reflect Affirm's underwriting choices as much as BNPL product mechanics.
3. **Seasonality in Affirm data.** Affirm's reported numbers are not seasonally adjusted. FRED's series are. Q1 and Q3 run slightly high in the raw Affirm data. This does not affect the peak comparison or the long-horizon correlation, but it would matter for a trader-grade analysis.
4. **Industry data not pulled.** The CFPB's 2022 and 2023 BNPL market reports provide industry-wide origination volume, dispute rates, and late-fee incidence. We cite them in the position memo but did not pull or process them in the pipeline.
